### FCAJ Community Day — June 2026 (livestream)

&emsp;**Date & time:** 13/06/2026

&emsp;**Location:** Community session of the First Cloud AI Journey program (AWS Study Group)

&emsp;**Role:** Attendee 

#### Event content

This community sharing session featured four presentations that combined real-world work experience, career development guidance, and system design on AWS. The topics ranged from the day-to-day responsibilities of a DevOps Engineer and the journey from being a First Cloud AI Journey participant to working in the AWS Partner ecosystem, to designing a scalable URL shortening service and understanding the mindset and culture required when working in multinational corporations. The speakers used their personal experiences and real-world situations to explain the topics, making the session more than just an introduction to technologies or job roles.

| # | Speaker | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trong H. Trương — DevOps Engineer, Endava Vietnam | What a DevOps Engineer Really Does and How to Start Learning DevOps |
| 2 | Danh Hoàng Hiếu Nghị — AI Engineer, AWS Community Builder | From First Cloud AI Journey to AWS Partner |
| 3 | Kiên và Thọ | Building a Scalable URL Shortening Service on AWS |
| 4 | Đạt Phạm — Data Analytics Engineer và Cường Nguyễn — Process Engineer | Career Stories and Corporate Culture in Multinational Companies |

The DevOps presentation distinguished common perceptions of DevOps—such as building CI/CD pipelines, using Docker and Kubernetes, or deploying applications—from the actual work, which requires a solid understanding of Linux, networking, containers, and the complete application lifecycle. The speaker also emphasized understanding the root causes of problems instead of simply copying commands, thinking in systems, automating repetitive tasks, and treating communication as an essential part of the role. Hieu Nghi's presentation described his journey from First Cloud AI Journey to the AWS Community Builder program and the AWS Partner ecosystem, showing that getting a job is only the beginning of a career path that can develop into many different roles. Kien and Tho presented how to build a scalable URL shortening service on AWS, covering URL identifier generation, URL mapping storage, high-traffic handling, and the balance between performance, reliability, and cost. The final presentation combined the perspectives of a Data Analytics Engineer and a Process Engineer to discuss working in multinational corporations, highlighting critical thinking, communication, data storytelling, and problem-solving skills. It also introduced a career development path from an executor to a systems thinker, as well as the importance of corporate culture and cultural fit in an international working environment.

#### Lessons and value gained

The DevOps presentation helped me realize that making a system work is only the first step; it is equally important to understand how an application is built, deployed, configured, and operated when failures occur. It encouraged me to learn Docker and AWS services by understanding their underlying mechanisms rather than simply copying commands. The journey from First Cloud AI Journey to AWS Partner also helped me view community participation and project work as opportunities to expand my knowledge, build connections, and explore career paths rather than merely adding items to my CV. The URL shortener presentation made me pay more attention to the design decisions behind seemingly simple features, especially data storage, scalability, performance, and cost as the number of users grows. I also connected these ideas to my team's project architecture, including the moderation history storage and RAG chatbot integration. Finally, the presentation on Data Analytics and corporate culture showed me that technical skills create value only when problems and solutions can be communicated clearly and when team members can collaborate effectively. The progression from an executor to a systems thinker helped me understand that my long-term goal should not be limited to completing assigned tasks but should also include understanding the broader context and proactively proposing improvements. I also want to apply the no-blame mindset in teamwork by focusing on the underlying causes within processes and systems rather than blaming an individual.
